Who I am

I’m 39, and I love telling people my age.

Not because age means nothing to me. Quite the opposite: I love the moment when the number doesn’t match what the other person sees in front of them.

But none of this happened by accident. I was never the girl who went to bed at ten, ate broccoli and meditated at sunrise. My life used to be parties, alcohol, Burning Man and Ibiza: a completely different lifestyle.

Six years ago I gave up alcohol completely and gradually started rebuilding everything else: food, movement, recovery, self-care and daily habits.

Today I train, teach Pilates and yoga, work with bodies professionally and keep refining my own system, built from what actually gets results.

I have two children, and I want to be with them properly, not in the gaps between workouts. So everything else has to fit into what’s left: an hour and a half a day, and not even in one go. That’s how my system was built: not for a free schedule nobody actually has, but for an ordinary day with almost no time for yourself.

In ninety days we’ll adapt this system to your life. My diet and my workouts were shaped by my body and my day; you need your own version, one that helps you look, move and feel younger.

Your 90-day path

Eight pillars that hold the result together

01

First, we understand where you’re starting from

We don’t start with a one-size-fits-all meal plan or ten thousand steps a day. I look closely at your story: lifestyle, food, sleep, work, stress, training, habits, past experience, what has worked before and what you’ve started and dropped many times.

Your body didn’t appear this morning: the way you look and feel today has been built over years from countless small decisions and circumstances.

02

We stop guessing and look at the data

If needed, you take an agreed set of blood tests, and we look at the markers that may be linked to how you feel, recover and perform. Anything medically out of range is discussed with the right specialist doctor.

“I’m lazy.” “I have no willpower.” “I just need to eat less.” “Why can everyone else do it but me?”

Some women blame themselves for being lazy for years, when part of the reason is physiological and shows up in the tests. So we look at the numbers first and draw conclusions second.

03

We go through your lifestyle

Youth doesn’t fit into a single jar of cream or a single exercise. It’s made of dozens of decisions we make every day: how we eat, move, sleep and recover, what we do with stress, how much we drink, how we look after ourselves and what happens on the days when we don’t feel like doing anything at all.

We find the choices that already work for you and the ones quietly working against you.

04

We create your Youth Map

After the first assessment I put together your personal system. It won’t be a list of fifty rules, just a few key levers that will make the biggest difference for you.

Food

What to change and how, without endless dieting or living with a calorie calculator.

Body

The kind of training your body needs right now.

Mind

The beliefs that shape how you treat yourself: comparing yourself to others, “it’s too late”, “I won’t have the willpower”. Without this one, the rest don’t last.

Recovery

Sleep, stress, rest, routine and the ability to recover after effort.

Appearance

Skin, posture, body composition and the other visible signs of age.

Habits

How to stop starting a new life every Monday and make the right actions automatic.

05

Your personal movement system

I don’t have one routine for everyone. Your plan may include strength work, Pilates, yoga, mobility, balance and coordination, neuromechanics and everyday movement, and the balance between them depends on your body.

I’m not attached to a single method. If you need Pilates, you’ll get Pilates; if you’re short on strength, we’ll build strength; and if your body first needs its mobility and confidence back, that’s where we’ll start.

06

We build your self-care routine

We go through what you’re using now and put together a clear home routine that fits your goals, lifestyle and budget.

We decide what’s worth doing at home regularly, what isn’t worth your money and what’s better left to a cosmetologist or dermatologist.

I make no secret of the fact that I use modern aesthetic treatments myself. So there’ll be no fairy tales here along the lines of “just drink water and you’ll never need Botox.”

07

We change habits instead of pretending to be perfect

This part matters to me most, because I know the difference between “knowing what’s right” and “actually changing your life.”

Giving up alcohol is the most obvious example. That big change didn’t happen because someone told me alcohol was bad for me: I knew that perfectly well already. Change happens when new choices become stronger than the old script.

What triggers your habits, where you keep derailing your own plans, what can change in your surroundings, and how to rebuild your life so that good choices take less and less heroism.

08

We make it work in real life

Making you perfect for ninety days doesn’t interest me. What matters far more is day ninety-one.

Your new system has to survive restaurants, travel, short nights, holidays, work, kids, bad moods and the days when you just can’t be bothered.

That’s why we build the programme into your real life, whatever it looks like.
